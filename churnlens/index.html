How to interpret the controls

More customers increase the expected number of churners; a higher at-risk mix raises churn probability; a higher monthly bill increases projected revenue at risk. They affect different parts of the estimate.

Elevated-risk customers are simulated with shorter tenure, more service friction, and declining use.
Revenue exposure uses the monthly amount multiplied by 12 months and expected churners.
Scenario assumption only; real offer effectiveness must be measured with an experiment.

Projected cohort outcomes

Expected-value estimates from the current model.

Not an actual company forecast
Increasing cohort size scales the expected churner count. Average revenue changes the estimated INR exposure. The churn probability is driven by risk characteristics—not by sample size alone.

Calculation

Transparent expected-value approach

1. ScoreModel scores each simulated customer profile.
2. AggregateExpected churners = sum of customer probabilities.
3. Apply actionEstimate effect for the portion receiving an offer.
4. ValueExpected exposure = churners × monthly revenue × 12.

Counts are expected values, so decimals can appear in the calculation. Displayed customer totals are rounded to whole people.

Model caveat

Use assumptions for learning and planning examples.

The scenario lab is not a causal experiment. A discount or support intervention may not reduce churn by the assumed amount. Real retention decisions should compare eligible customers with an appropriate control group and include incentive cost, fairness, and customer consent.
